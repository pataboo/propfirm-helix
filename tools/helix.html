Why it exists

Comparison sites show a price and a star rating. Neither tells you whether the price is the list fee or a promotion, whether the firm forbids the automation you plan to run, or whether the reviews behind the score came from verified customers. Helix records those separately and leaves a gap where the evidence does not exist, rather than filling it.

What a table cannot do

  • Goal finder — capital, budget, market and strategy become a shortlist, not a search box.
  • Family plan — how many people, how much each, total capital, total fees, which copy rules break, and how long until the first payout.
  • Rule verdicts — EA, news, overnight, weekend and copy trading recorded separately for the evaluation and the funded phase.
  • Multiple ratings — PropFirmMatch, Trustpilot, Sitejabber and Feefo side by side, each linking to its source, with Trustpilot warnings kept visible.
  • Execution — the brokers that can actually run the platform a challenge uses.

Where the data comes from

  • Each firm's own pricing page — read programme by programme, size by size, with the verbatim sentence kept.
  • PropFirmMatch — payout records, leverage tables, partner codes, review counts.
  • Trustpilot — rating, review count, and the profiles where Trustpilot withholds a score.
  • Sitejabber / Feefo — secondary ratings, thin coverage in this industry, shown as such.

What it does not claim

Helix is a record of what firms publish — not an audit, and not investment advice. Prices move: every row shows when it was checked, and rows we could not verify are marked instead of guessed. Ratings are third-party opinions, not our assessment of a firm's solvency or honesty.
