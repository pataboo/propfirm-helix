Short answer

Should you start a prop firm challenge? Yes — if you can afford to lose the fee, and not before you have read the rules of the firm you are paying.

  • Treat the fee as tuition, not as an investment. Most people fail. A published sample of 300,000 accounts across ten firms put the pass rate at about 14% and payouts at about 7%.
  • Read the rules before the price. Daily loss, trailing drawdown and news restrictions decide whether your method survives. Cost is the last thing to compare.
  • Do not take our advice. Take our data. We have not passed a challenge yet. What we can offer is a database where the empty cells are labelled as empty.

01 What this is

Clear about both sides of the line.

It is not

  • A promise of returns, or advice that you may not lose money.

02 The real math

A $99 challenge is not a $99 decision.

At a 14% pass rate, the average trader buys about seven challenges before one passes. The fee is the smallest part of what you are risking.

Pass rate source: published aggregate of ~300,000 accounts at ten firms (thepropfirmguide.com, 2026). Firm-level rates differ widely.

03 Build order

The order of the decisions, not of our enthusiasm.

  1. live01 Where to trade — Helix: cost, rules and break-even per firm
  2. live02 What you may not break — rule sets turned into per-firm checklists
  3. building03 What you actually trade — strategy lab: backtests, walk-forward, what survives
  4. planned04 How you execute — tools, checklists, decision journal
  5. planned05 What it produced — attempts, fees, payouts, capital ladder

04 Process

Four steps, in this order.

01 · Read before you payRules, drawdown type, payout cycle. If a firm will not publish a number, we leave the cell empty rather than guess.
02 · Write the method downEntry, exit, risk per trade, what stops you. If it cannot be written in one page, it is not a method yet.
03 · Test on your own dataBacktests are cheap. Walk-forward is honest. Anything that only survives one market regime is a wish.
04 · Then, and only then, payOne challenge, smallest size, treated as a cost. The journal — not the result — decides whether to continue.

05 FAQ

Questions people actually ask.

Are you funded?

No. We say it on the front page because it is the most important thing about this site. Helix exists so that the first paid challenge is an informed decision.

Do you sell signals or a course?

No. There is nothing to buy here. The database is public, the method is written in the open, and the letters are free.

Why is Helix inside SilentQuants instead of next to it?

Because you cannot choose a firm without the numbers, and you cannot trade without the rules. The database is the first layer of the work, not a separate product.

Where does the data come from?

Every row points at its source: the firm's own page where we could read it, or a comparison site where we could not. Each row carries a grade (A for confirmed from the firm, C for unconfirmed) and a date.

What happens when a number is missing?

The cell stays empty and says so. We would rather show a gap than a number nobody can check — that is the whole reason this database exists.

How often is it updated?

Weekly, when there is something to change. The build date is printed on the page, and rows carry their own verification date.
