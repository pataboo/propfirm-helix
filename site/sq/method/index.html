Method · how we work

A method we can write down
is the only one we trust.

Four steps, in this order. If a step cannot be finished, the next one does not start — especially the one that costs money.

01 The four steps

Read, write, test, then pay.

01 · Read before you payRules, drawdown type and payout cycle, read from the firm's own page. If a firm will not publish a number, we leave the cell empty rather than guess.
02 · Write the method downEntry, exit, risk per trade, and what stops you. If it cannot fit on one page, it is not a method yet — it is a mood.
03 · Test on your own dataBacktests are cheap, walk-forward is honest. Anything that only survives one market regime is a wish.
04 · Then, and only then, payOne challenge, smallest size, treated as a cost. The journal — not the result — decides whether to continue.

02 Rules we do not bend

Why each one exists.

Evidence over opinion.A result counts only when someone else can check it. When we don't know, we say so. Because an unchecked number feels exactly like a checked one.
One variable at a time.Change the stop, or the filter, or the session — never all three between two tests. Because a large change hides the one mistake that caused it.
Small, paid proof.Conviction is not a reason to skip the cheapest possible attempt at proving it. Because the market charges for lessons either way — we would rather choose the price.

03 The real math

What a challenge costs before it is a challenge.

At a 14% pass rate the average trader buys about seven challenges before one passes. A $99 fee is roughly $707 of expected spend before a single payout — and that excludes monthly fees and the weeks each attempt takes.
